# ADR-0005: Elicitation layer restructuring — topic library, decision-node graduation, and tournament mechanism

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-07-26
**Deciders:** Bill

## Context

`reference/elicitation.md` and `design/architecture.md` §1.1 currently treat three things as structurally separate: **decision nodes** (binary/near-binary forks, resolved by the interview module, §1 of that file), **priorities** (target/scalar/polarity-shaped, weighed against each other by the values pass's pairwise tournament, §2), and **category filters** (a fixed set of discrete labels, where the candidate states an acceptable subset and a ranking directly — career phase and employment type are the two instances currently named). A multi-turn design discussion, prompted by "the initial script hard-wired in specific assumptions," examined whether this three-way split holds up, whether a YAML-style config should separate the elicitation *method* from elicitation *content*, and whether a standardized-instrument-style validity/consistency check is worth building in. Working through JobMux's own real example (Hannah's case-1 search) surfaced that the split doesn't hold up cleanly: a geographic decision node that resolves as "willing to commute" has stopped being binary and become a scalar with a threshold; a further "willing to travel overnight, or nationally, or internationally — which would she prefer" is a category filter; "considering more than one place to live, which would she prefer" is architecture.md's own already-named "open to more than one [target], ranked" case. None of these fit "decision node" as originally scoped, and all three are preference questions the existing tournament machinery is specifically built to answer more reliably than direct self-report.

## Decision

**1. A decision node that doesn't resolve as a clean hard line graduates into the values-pass machinery**, rather than staying narrowed-but-unintegrated inside the interview module. `reference/elicitation.md` §1.3 step 4 already anticipates a decision node resolving as "a hard boundary vs. a gradated tolerance vs. a genuine toss-up" — what was missing is what happens next. Once gradated, it gets a real shape (scalar, most often, per Hannah's commute-tolerance example), a threshold, and a tournament-derived weight against the candidate's other priorities, the same as any other priority. The interview module's job is recognizing that graduation needs to happen; it does not do the weighing itself.

**2. Category filters with 3+ options, and any graduated decision node with 3+ candidate alternatives, get tournament-derived rankings instead of a direct "which would you take first" self-report.** Architecture.md's own reasoning for the priority tournament — a direct rank/weight is less reliable than one derived from forced-choice comparisons — applies exactly as much to a location-arrangement menu (local-only / commutable / overnight-travel / national / international) or multiple candidate home locations as it does to priorities. (A 2-option category filter is already a single pairwise comparison; nothing changes there.) One shared tournament mechanism serves priorities within a dimension, dimensions against each other, graduated decision nodes, and category filters uniformly — not a bespoke ranking method per shape.

**3. Tournament pairing strategy: information-gain-driven adaptive pairing**, generalizing `design/architecture.md` §1.5's existing information-gain-per-cost ordering (currently used to decide what to check next in evidence-gathering) one level down to deciding which pair to compare next in a tournament. At each step, the next pair queried is the one expected to most reduce uncertainty about the current top-two-or-three boundary, following the Bradley-Terry active-querying approach of Chen, Bennett, Collins-Thompson & Horvitz's "Pairwise Ranking Aggregation in a Crowdsourced Setting" (Crowd-BT, WSDM 2013) rather than round-robin or a fixed pairing schedule. `jobmux/tournament.py`'s existing `bradley_terry_scores` already tolerates a sparse, disconnected comparison graph by design — this decision specifies what actually selects which sparse subset of pairs gets compared, which nothing in the codebase decides today.

**4. Closing safety net, not full round-robin, addresses the "did we miss a real contender" risk.** `reference/elicitation.md` §2.6's existing closing pattern ("So for <dimension>, it sounds like <top priority> matters most... Sound right?") gets one addition: explicitly name whichever candidate items the abbreviated tournament never compared, and ask directly whether any of them are actually contenders. This is a small extension of an already-written pattern, not a new mechanism, and it resolves the adaptive-pairing failure mode (an early, noisy comparison wrongly burying a real contender) at the cost of one closing question instead of full exhaustive comparison.

**5. Interview depth is a candidate-set, revisable dial — brief vs. in-depth — distinct from the two budget axes architecture.md already has** (priority weights over outcomes, §1.1; cost as a constraint on the evidence-gathering process, §1.5). This is candidate-time cost during the interview itself. Brief mode: skip the aptitude module by default, cap decision-node graduation to a lighter touch, use the cheaper tournament-execution path (below). In-depth mode: full aptitude module, full graduation, and — per the discussion's own resolution — no need to fall back to full round-robin even here, since the closing safety net (4) already covers the risk full round-robin would otherwise guard against.

**6. Survey pre-fill strengthens a hypothesis; it does not confirm one.** For decision nodes, a survey answer becomes a stronger prior for `reference/elicitation.md` §1.3's existing "state the candidate's own fork back to them as a hypothesis" pattern — the existing confirm-or-revise protocol is already the proving step; survey data just replaces an invented cold-start guess with a real one. For priorities, the tournament itself is what proves a survey-stated importance level — a survey saying "compensation matters most" is a hypothesis, not a finding, until the tournament's derived rank either matches it or doesn't.

**7. The trigger for a validity/consistency check is mechanical, not a model-judged frequency decision.** Rather than asking the model to decide how often to probe for contradictions — an unbounded judgment call with no natural stopping rule, the same shape as the tournament's own not-yet-fixed stopping threshold (`reference/elicitation.md` §7) — the check fires only on a detectable mismatch between two independently-obtained signals about the same thing: a survey-stated or interview-stated importance level vs. the tournament-derived rank for that same priority, or a survey-supplied answer vs. a later live-confirmed one. Detecting the mismatch is a cheap diff, structurally the same shape as `jobmux/verification.py`'s existing `diff_ledger_value` and conceptually the same discipline as `jobmux/signal_fusion.py`'s two-signals-can-disagree reconciliation, just applied to preference data instead of listing evidence. In the common case where nothing disagrees, this costs zero extra questions.

**8. The YAML topic library supplies candidates, not the flow.** `reference/elicitation.md` stays the fixed, human-reviewed method — the actual verbatim conversational mechanics (one question per turn, the decision-node confirmation protocol, the shape-determination questions, the tournament turn patterns) do not move into YAML and are not synthesized by the model at runtime. The YAML holds a per-topic library entry: topic name/id, which module it typically first surfaces in, its known shape or shape-progression (geography commonly starts as a decision node, commonly resolves to a scalar-with-threshold, sometimes further decomposes into a category filter — recorded as an actual pattern now that Hannah's case demonstrated it, not invented), a known candidate option set where the topic is category-filter-shaped, and satisfaction criteria for when it counts as resolved. This is explicitly a recognition library the model draws on, never a mandatory checklist — a topic being in the library is not itself evidence the candidate cares about it, the same discipline the current values-pass starter set already states ("adjusted for domain... let the candidate add, rename, or discard").

**9. Aptitude gets a narrow, self-rating-triggered consistency probe, kept conceptually distinct from an adjacent enjoyment/preference question even though the two are deliberately sequenced near each other in conversation.** "You rated both group and solo work exceptional — which are you actually better at?" is a legitimate light validity check on the aptitude self-rating specifically (worth asking mainly on a self-rated tie, not systematically for every aptitude pair, since architecture.md is explicit that aptitudes are nonpolar and being exceptional at both is a coherent answer). "Which do you enjoy more?" is not an aptitude question by the existing definitions at all — it's a values-pass priority question, and gets routed there. The two are asked as naturally adjacent turns, not the same turn, and not necessarily in the same module-ordered position `reference/elicitation.md` currently implies (§1 through §6 read as a strict sequence) — a good interview lets an aptitude answer motivate an adjacent priority question immediately, then returns to systematic coverage, the same "follow the candidate's thread" discipline the interview module already claims for itself, just extended across module boundaries.

**10. The tournament-execution loop, once topics are already named, can run at algorithmic-plus-small-model tier under an explicit low-frontier-budget preference — this narrows, rather than contradicts, architecture.md §1.5's existing "the interview itself" exception to the minimize-frontier-inference floor.** That exception (§1.5, §2.3) is about holding revisable hypotheses over open-ended conversation and judging resolved-vs-genuinely-open — real judgment work. Once a topic list already exists (which does require that frontier-tier work), running the tournament comparisons is different in kind: pair selection is already decided as algorithmic (3, above); phrasing "between X and Y, which matters more?" from an already-named pair is a fixed template needing no model call at all; and parsing which side a candidate's answer picked is a bounded extraction task, the same shape §2.3 already calls small-model-plausible elsewhere. Frontier-tier judgment only re-enters if an answer comes back genuinely ambiguous rather than cleanly mapping to one side. This tiering only activates on an explicit candidate-set low-budget preference, never as a silent default, consistent with every other tier-downgrade rule already in this project.

## Options Considered

### Issue 1 — decision-node/priority boundary

**Option A — keep decision nodes and priorities structurally separate always (status quo, not adopted).** Simpler mental model, but demonstrably wrong against JobMux's own real case-1 example — a gradated geography constraint has nowhere principled to live if it can never become a real, tournament-weighed priority.

**Option B — graduate a gradated decision node into the values pass (adopted).** Matches what §1.3 step 4 already gestures at without finishing the thought. The interview module discovers and characterizes the shape; the values pass does the actual weighing — division of labor stays clean, just extended one step further than it currently is written.

**Option C — spicy, considered and not adopted: abolish the decision-node/priority distinction entirely, treat every candidate concern as a priority from the start, tournament-weighed, with "hard line" reduced to a threshold set at the extreme end of a scale.** Maximally uniform — one mechanism for everything, no graduation logic needed at all. Not adopted: a genuine hard binary (a caregiving schedule constraint that's truly non-negotiable, not just strongly preferred) loses real information if it's forced through scalar/threshold machinery built for gradated preferences — `design/architecture.md` §1.1's own mandatory-flag-for-targets mechanism exists precisely because a target has "no natural continuum to hang a graded cutoff on," and collapsing that distinction to simplify the elicitation layer would re-introduce a problem the values-pass design already solved once.

### Issue 2 — tournament pairing strategy

**Option A — Swiss-style pairing by current-estimate proximity.** Cheap, well-understood, the closest literal match to a classic Swiss tournament. The cheap heuristic approximation of Option C below — pairs by distance in current estimate rather than by computed expected information gain, which is unreliable early when estimates themselves are still noisy.

**Option B — minimum-comparison top-k selection (classical tournament/selection algorithms).** Most directly targeted at "we only need the top two or three, not a full sort" — real, comparison-minimizing algorithms exist for exactly this. Not adopted as the primary mechanism: a bracket/elimination structure recovers a clean top-*k* but discards most of the relative-strength information among the rest of the set, and `design/architecture.md` §1.1's reallocation rule needs real weights for every priority in a dimension, not just a cutoff line.

**Option C — information-gain-driven adaptive pairing, Crowd-BT-style (adopted).** Chen, Bennett, Collins-Thompson & Horvitz, "Pairwise Ranking Aggregation in a Crowdsourced Setting" (WSDM 2013) — an online Bradley-Terry model that queries whichever pair maximizes expected information gain about the ranking, particularly efficient at resolving the top items rather than a full order. Directly reuses `design/architecture.md` §1.5's existing information-gain-per-cost principle one level down, rather than introducing an unrelated concept.

**Option D — spicy, considered and not adopted as the default: full round-robin, no adaptive pairing at all.** Justified by `reference/verification-escalation-policy.md` §5's existing "rigor is never traded for time or budget" — any adaptive-selection scheme has a real failure mode (an early noisy comparison rationally deciding further comparison on an item isn't worth it, permanently under-ranking something that wasn't actually weak), which is a machine-generated version of exactly the "confirmation counts, don't assume" failure this project polices everywhere else. Not adopted as the default because the closing safety net (Decision 4) addresses the same risk far more cheaply than exhaustive comparison; reserved conceptually for a candidate who wants maximum rigor over speed, though even in-depth mode doesn't require it once the safety net exists.

### Issue 3 — survey pre-fill discipline

**Option A — spicy, considered and not adopted: treat a completed survey answer as already-confirmed, skip live discussion of that topic entirely.** Fastest possible interview for anything the survey covers. Not adopted: directly contradicts this project's single most consistent discipline, restated in nearly every reference doc — "confirmation counts; the brief said so does not." A survey answer is real information but was not tested against a live trade-off the way the interview's own confirmation protocol requires.

**Option B — treat survey data as no different from a cold-start guess, always run the full interview from scratch (not adopted).** Wastes real information the candidate already provided. Also risks feeling repetitive or inattentive to the candidate ("didn't I already tell you this").

**Option C — survey strengthens the hypothesis, live process still proves it (adopted).** For decision nodes: a stronger prior into the existing confirm-or-revise protocol. For priorities: the tournament is the actual proof, not a direct confirmation question — this is what makes Decision 7's mechanical mismatch-detection meaningful rather than redundant with a simple "is this still true?" check.

### Issue 4 — YAML topic-library scope

**Option A — YAML as pure data, elicitation.md stays the fixed method (adopted).** Consistent with the project's established template-instantiation discipline everywhere else — the model fills slots from data, it does not write new conversational flow from a specification.

**Option B — spicy, considered and not adopted: YAML "is the whole script for the script," elicitation.md becomes a generic topic-driven-interview engine with no fixed per-topic wording at all.** Maximally flexible and easy to extend with new topics without touching the reviewed prose. Not adopted: this asks the model to synthesize conversational flow and wording from structured data at runtime rather than running fixed, human-reviewed text — exactly the free-synthesis drift risk `design/architecture.md` §2.1 names as the reason template-instantiation is used everywhere else in this project.

**Option C — a middle ground, discussed but not separately adopted or rejected in this pass: one versioned topic-library pack per domain/sector, rather than one global library.** Would let someone ship a "nursing topics" pack without touching the core script. Not resolved here — recorded as a plausible future refinement of Decision 8's shape once a real second domain (per `design/concept.md` §4.3's sector backlog) is actually being onboarded, not decided speculatively now.

## Trade-off Analysis

Issues 1 and 2 both resolve the same way: extend an existing mechanism (the values pass, the tournament) to cover cases the original scoping didn't anticipate, rather than either freezing the original scope (Option A in both cases) or dissolving useful distinctions entirely for uniformity's sake (Issue 1's Option C). Issue 2's pairing-strategy choice mirrors `jobmux/scoring.py`'s and `jobmux/signal_fusion.py`'s own pattern of citing a real, established technique rather than inventing one — Crowd-BT is doing here what Dempster-Shafer combination and augmented weighted Tchebycheff scalarization already did for their respective modules.

Issue 3 is really about which of two already-established project disciplines wins when they'd otherwise pull in different directions: efficiency (skip what's already known) vs. "confirmation counts" (nothing is resolved until the candidate's own live words say so). The adopted option doesn't split the difference — it keeps "confirmation counts" fully intact and gets the efficiency gain somewhere else entirely (fewer *new* questions needed, since the tournament that would run anyway is what does the proving, not an extra dedicated confirmation question).

Issue 4 is the same free-synthesis-vs-template-instantiation tension this project has resolved the same way in every other reference doc; Option B would have been the first real reversal of that discipline, which is why it's marked spicy rather than a live contender.

## Consequences

- `design/architecture.md` §1.1 (decision node vs. priority scoping), §1.5 (the "interview itself" frontier-tier exception), and §2.3 (tournament-related task tiering) all need revision to reflect Decisions 1, 8, and 10 above — not done as part of writing this ADR. `reference/elicitation.md` needs a substantial revision: a new graduation protocol (Decision 1), tournament-derived rather than direct-report rankings for category filters (Decision 2), the closing safety-net addition (Decision 4), a brief/in-depth mode distinction threaded throughout (Decision 5), and the aptitude-bridging pattern (Decision 9). None of this is implemented by this ADR.
- `jobmux/tournament.py` needs new code: the actual pairing-selection algorithm (Decision 3) doesn't exist yet — only `bradley_terry_scores`, which consumes whatever pairs it's given. This is real, testable, algorithmic-tier work for a future session, not a design decision left open — the *design* is decided (Crowd-BT-style information-gain pairing); the *code* is not written.
- The YAML topic library (Decision 8) doesn't exist as a file yet — its schema (topic id, originating module, shape/shape-progression, candidate option set, satisfaction criteria) is specified in Decision 8 above but not yet built or populated.
- `reference/elicitation.md` §7's still-open tournament-stopping-threshold question is now explicitly coupled to Decision 3's pairing strategy (an information-gain criterion naturally suggests its own stopping rule — stop once no remaining pair's expected information gain exceeds some threshold — but this ADR does not pin that threshold, consistent with how `jobmux/scoring.py`'s and `jobmux/signal_fusion.py`'s own first-instantiation parameters were left open rather than hardcoded).
- `decisions/README.md`'s index needs updating (done as part of writing this ADR, per established practice).
